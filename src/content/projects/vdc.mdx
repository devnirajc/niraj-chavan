The corporate website for Deshmukh & Deshpande Associates, a firm of chartered accountants: its services, news, articles, careers and a knowledge base for clients. I built it at Whitecode between late 2014 and 2015, in the first year of my career. The site is no longer online.

- **Client:** Deshmukh & Deshpande Associates, chartered accountants
- **Industry:** accounting and professional services
- **Product:** the firm's public website
- **Users:** prospective clients looking for services, existing clients reading articles and the knowledge base, job applicants, and the firm's own staff publishing content
- **My role:** Software Developer at Whitecode. I built the site on WordPress
- **When:** 2014 – 2015

## The business problem

A chartered-accountancy firm is judged on trust and expertise before a client ever calls. The firm needed a website that did four jobs:

- **Explain what the firm does**, with a page for each practice area.
- **Show its expertise**, through news, events, articles and a knowledge base clients could come back to.
- **Show who it is**: its leadership, team, offices, and its ethics and staff policies.
- **Turn interest into contact**: a contact form for enquiries and a careers page for applicants.

Much of that content changes all the time. News, articles and knowledge-base entries are written by the firm, not by a developer, so the firm's staff had to be able to publish it themselves.

## Architecture and design

The site runs on **WordPress**, on the Spacious theme by ThemeGrill, with plugins for the contact form, team pages, tabbed content and a photo gallery. It is organised into seven sections:

- Service pages for each practice area
- News and events
- Blog and articles
- A knowledge base
- Careers
- Contact
- The firm's profile: leadership, team, offices, ethics and staff policies

### Why WordPress

The site is mostly content that the firm itself keeps adding to. WordPress gave the firm's staff an editor they could publish news, articles and knowledge-base entries in, without asking a developer. An established theme and well-used plugins covered the presentation, contact form, team pages and gallery.

## Looking back

This was one of my first projects, and I'd approach parts of it differently today:

- **Accessibility from the first commit.** I would test the theme, forms and tabbed content with a keyboard and a screen reader, and aim for WCAG 2.2 AA, as I do on what I build now.
- **A plan for maintenance.** A WordPress site depends on regular core, theme and plugin updates. Today I would agree who owns those updates before handover, so the site stays secure after the project ends.
- **Performance budgets.** Image optimisation, caching and a measured page weight for a site whose visitors judge the firm on first impression.
- **Search over the knowledge base**, so clients can find an answer without browsing article by article.

## Outcome summary

The firm got a website that presented its practice areas, team and expertise, took enquiries and applications, and let its own staff publish news, articles and knowledge-base content. For me, projects like this at Whitecode were the start of a path that runs through e-commerce, enterprise Angular applications and, today, Chase Travel at JP Morgan Chase.
