The Field Logistics Module (FLM) is the Angular application Schlumberger, now SLB, uses to manage its global logistics inventory for field operations. More than 500 engineers in several regions use it. I worked on it as a UI developer at Schlumberger from 2019 to 2021. Three pieces of work there were mine: the real-time inventory-tracking dashboard, the automated test suites, and the upgrade from Angular 8 to Angular 11.

FLM is internal enterprise software, so there is no public demo or repository. This write-up describes patterns and outcomes, not internal systems.

- **Organisation:** Schlumberger (SLB), oilfield services
- **Product:** internal logistics-inventory application for field operations
- **Users:** 500+ engineers across several geographies
- **My role:** UI Developer. I built the real-time inventory dashboard and the unit and end-to-end test suites, and I led the Angular 8 → 11 upgrade
- **When:** 2019 – 2021

## The business problem

Three problems shaped my work on FLM:

- **Inventory discrepancies.** FLM's inventory records didn't always match what was actually there. This was the measure the real-time dashboard was later judged on.
- **A three-day regression cycle.** A full QA regression pass took three days.
- **An ageing framework.** FLM ran on Angular 8. Angular 11 shipped in November 2020, the same month Angular 8's long-term support ended.

## Architecture and design

FLM is an Angular single-page application written in TypeScript. The real-time inventory dashboard is built on Kibana-integrated data feeds. Quality is enforced at two levels: Jasmine and Karma unit tests on components and services, and WebDriver end-to-end tests that drive the running application, with results reported through Allure.

## Key technical challenges

### Keeping inventory views close to reality

**Challenge.** FLM's inventory records and the physical stock they described drifted apart.

**Solution.** I built a real-time inventory-tracking dashboard on Kibana-integrated data feeds, so the current state of inventory was visible as it changed.

**Outcome.** Within one quarter of go-live, inventory discrepancies fell by about 35%.

### Turning a three-day regression cycle into hours

**Challenge.** Every full regression pass took three days.

**Solution.** I took unit-test coverage to about 80% with Jasmine and Karma, and built a full end-to-end suite with WebDriver, with results published as Allure reports.

**Outcome.** With both suites in place, a regression cycle that took three days took under six hours, short enough to finish within a working day.

### Upgrading Angular 8 to 11 with zero downtime

**Challenge.** Moving a production application with 500+ users across three major Angular versions without disrupting the people who depended on it.

**Analysis.** Angular's update tooling moves one major version at a time, so going from 8 to 11 meant crossing three major releases. The largest change sat in the middle: Angular 9 made Ivy the default compiler and renderer, replacing the View Engine FLM was built on.

**Solution.** I led the upgrade: I planned the migration, coordinated it with QA and rolled it out to production.

**Outcome.** FLM moved from Angular 8 to Angular 11 with zero production downtime.

## Business impact

- **More reliable inventory:** about 35% fewer inventory discrepancies within one quarter of the dashboard going live.
- **Faster regression testing:** a full regression pass went from three days to under six hours.
- **A supported platform:** FLM moved to Angular 11 with zero production downtime for its 500+ users.

## Leadership and collaboration

I led the Angular upgrade from start to finish: I planned the migration and coordinated it with QA through to the production rollout.

## Outcome summary

- **Key achievements:** a real-time inventory dashboard, about 80% unit-test coverage plus a full end-to-end suite, and a three-version Angular upgrade with zero downtime.
- **Business benefit:** fewer inventory discrepancies, and a regression cycle short enough to finish within a working day.
- **Long-term value:** an automated safety net that every later release could run, on an Angular version that was still supported.
