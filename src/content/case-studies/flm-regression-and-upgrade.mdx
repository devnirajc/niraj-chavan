## Context

The Field Logistics Module (FLM) is the Angular application Schlumberger, now SLB, uses to manage its global logistics inventory for field operations. More than 500 engineers in several regions use it. I worked on it as a UI developer from 2019 to 2021.

Two problems shaped this work, and they are connected:

- **A three-day regression cycle.** A full QA regression pass took three days of testing time.
- **An ageing framework.** FLM ran on Angular 8. Angular 11 shipped in November 2020, the same month Angular 8's long-term support ended.

A slow regression cycle makes every release expensive, and a framework upgrade is the biggest regression risk an application takes on. Fast, trustworthy tests are what make an upgrade like this safe to ship.

### Two layers of tests, each doing a different job

The two suites check different things:

- **Unit tests** check components, services and pipes in isolation. They run in seconds and point at the exact function that broke, which makes them the right place for business rules and edge cases.
- **End-to-end tests** drive a real browser through the application's user journeys. They are slower, but they catch what unit tests can't see: routing, navigation and the wiring between components and pages.
- **Allure reports** turn a test run into something QA can read: which journeys passed, which failed and where, without digging through console logs.

A full regression pass that took three days now finishes in under six hours, short enough to run within a working day.

### Why Angular 8 → 11 was more than a version bump

Angular's update tooling moves one major version at a time, so going from 8 to 11 meant crossing three major releases. The largest change sat in the middle: Angular 9 made Ivy the default compiler and renderer, replacing the View Engine that FLM was built on. Libraries compiled for View Engine had to go through Angular's compatibility compiler. Angular 10 and 11 brought newer TypeScript versions, stricter defaults and deprecated APIs to clear out along the way.

I planned the migration, coordinated it with QA, and rolled it out to production with zero downtime for FLM's 500+ users.

## Results

- **Regression in hours, not days:** a full regression pass went from three days to under six hours.
- **Coverage:** about 80% unit-test coverage with Jasmine and Karma, alongside a full WebDriver end-to-end suite reported through Allure.
- **A supported platform:** FLM moved from Angular 8 to Angular 11, back onto a version that receives fixes, with zero production downtime.
